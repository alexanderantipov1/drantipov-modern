Surgical Wisdom Tooth Removal Explained: Why Dentists Section Impacted Wisdom Teeth

Hearing that you need a wisdom tooth removed can be intimidating—especially if your dentist tells you the tooth is impacted and requires surgery.

Fortunately, modern surgical techniques make impacted wisdom tooth removal safe, predictable, and far less traumatic than many people expect.

One of the most effective techniques used during surgical extractions is called tooth sectioning. Rather than removing the entire tooth at once, the tooth is carefully divided into smaller sections that can be removed individually. This approach minimizes unnecessary pressure on the jawbone and surrounding tissues while allowing the surgeon to remove the tooth with greater precision.

Here's how the procedure works and why it is often the preferred technique for impacted wisdom teeth.

What Is an Impacted Wisdom Tooth?

Wisdom teeth are the last permanent teeth to develop and typically erupt between the ages of 17 and 25.

In many cases, there isn't enough room in the jaw for these teeth to fully emerge. As a result, they may remain partially or completely trapped beneath the gums or bone. This is known as an impacted wisdom tooth.

Impacted wisdom teeth can lead to several problems, including:

Pain and swelling
Gum infections
Damage to neighboring teeth
Tooth decay
Cysts or other complications
Difficulty cleaning the area

When these problems occur—or are likely to develop—surgical extraction is often recommended.

Why Isn't the Tooth Removed in One Piece?

Many patients assume a wisdom tooth is simply pulled out.

For impacted teeth, however, that is often neither possible nor ideal.

Because the tooth is firmly surrounded by bone and soft tissue, attempting to remove it in one piece could require removing excessive bone or applying unnecessary force to the jaw.

Instead, oral surgeons frequently use a technique called tooth sectioning.

By dividing the tooth into smaller pieces, each section can be removed individually through a smaller opening, reducing trauma to the surrounding structures.

Step 1: Accessing the Tooth

The procedure begins after the area has been completely numbed with local anesthesia. Depending on the complexity of the case and the patient's comfort level, sedation may also be used.

A small incision is made in the gum tissue to expose the impacted wisdom tooth.

If necessary, a minimal amount of bone is carefully removed to provide safe access to the tooth.

Step 2: Sectioning the Tooth

Once the tooth is visible, the surgeon uses a specialized surgical handpiece to divide it into smaller sections.

The exact number of sections depends on factors such as:

The position of the tooth
Root shape
Bone density
Degree of impaction

This controlled approach allows the surgeon to remove each portion individually while protecting nearby teeth, nerves, and surrounding bone.

Step 3: Removing Each Section

After sectioning, the larger portions of the tooth are carefully elevated and removed first.

The remaining sections are then extracted one at a time.

Removing the tooth in stages gives the surgeon greater control throughout the procedure and helps minimize stress on the jaw.

Step 4: Root Tip Removal

Once the crown has been removed, any remaining root tips are carefully located and extracted.

This is one of the most delicate parts of the procedure because preserving healthy bone and protecting nearby anatomical structures is essential.

The surgical site is thoroughly inspected to ensure no tooth fragments remain.

Step 5: Cleaning the Surgical Site

Before closing the incision, the extraction socket is irrigated to remove small debris and ensure the area is clean.

This step helps prepare the site for proper healing.

Step 6: Suturing the Incision

After the tooth has been completely removed, the gum tissue is repositioned and secured with sutures.

These stitches help:

Protect the surgical site
Control bleeding
Support healing
Reduce the risk of food becoming trapped in the extraction area

Depending on the type of sutures used, they may dissolve on their own or be removed during a follow-up appointment.

What Happens After Surgery?

Most patients experience some swelling, mild discomfort, and limited jaw stiffness during the first few days after surgery.

Your oral surgeon may recommend:

Prescription or over-the-counter pain medication
Ice packs during the first 24 hours
Soft foods
Plenty of fluids
Gentle oral hygiene around the surgical site

If appropriate, antibiotics may also be prescribed to reduce the risk of infection.

Following your post-operative instructions carefully is one of the most important factors in achieving a smooth recovery.

Is Tooth Sectioning Safe?

Yes.

Tooth sectioning is a well-established oral surgery technique used every day by experienced surgeons around the world.

Although dividing the tooth into smaller pieces may sound more complicated, it is often the safest and least traumatic method for removing impacted wisdom teeth.

The technique allows for:

Smaller surgical openings
Less bone removal
More controlled extraction
Reduced stress on surrounding teeth
Greater surgical precision
When Should Impacted Wisdom Teeth Be Removed?

Not every wisdom tooth needs to be extracted.

However, removal is often recommended when impacted teeth are causing pain, infection, damage to neighboring teeth, or other oral health concerns.

A comprehensive examination, including digital X-rays or 3D imaging, allows your oral surgeon to determine whether surgical removal is the best option for your situation.
