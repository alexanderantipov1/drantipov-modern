### The “Teeth in a Day” Concept Explained

In a same-day workflow, teeth that can’t be preserved may be removed, implants are placed, and a temporary bridge may be attached during the same visit. Immediate restoration is appropriate only when factors such as overall health and implant stability support it. The temporary bridge provides fixed teeth while the implants heal, but it is not the final prosthesis or proof that healing is complete. Follow-up care and your surgeon’s instructions on eating and cleaning remain essential.

**Immediate-load implants** are implants fitted with a temporary restoration soon after placement. Outcomes depend on individual factors such as health, bone conditions, implant stability, and the treatment protocol, so a general figure can’t predict an individual result. ### All-on-4 vs. Traditional Implants

Traditional full-arch treatment may use a staged process, with implants placed first and teeth attached after a healing period. All-on-4 can shorten the time to a fixed temporary bridge for suitable patients, but surgery still requires recovery, and healing varies. Four implants can support a full arch when their position, bone support, and bite forces are carefully planned. Six or eight implants may be appropriate in other cases. A higher implant count alone doesn’t guarantee a better result.

The two rear implants are often angled to make use of available bone and create support for a longer bridge. Their orientation can help distribute biting forces and may reduce the need for extensive bone grafting in some treatment plans. It isn’t a universal substitute for grafting: significant bone loss or other anatomical factors can change the approach. Imaging and a clinical assessment guide the decision.

“All-on-X” describes the broader principle of securing a full-arch bridge to a customized number of implants. The “X” varies with a patient’s anatomy, bite, and restorative plan. The goal isn’t simply to use the fewest implants possible. It’s to select a stable design that suits the individual and supports long-term care.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long do All-on-4 dental implants last?

All-on-4 implants can serve for many years, but no specific lifespan is guaranteed. The implants and the attached bridge are separate components, and their care needs can differ. Daily cleaning, routine professional checkups, bite changes, health conditions, and habits such as smoking may affect their long-term condition. If the bridge feels loose, your bite changes, or cleaning becomes difficult, arrange an evaluation rather than waiting for a regular visit.

### Is the All-on-4 procedure painful with IV sedation?

IV sedation can help reduce awareness and anxiety, while local anesthetic numbs the surgical area; sedation doesn’t mean you’ll feel no sensations or discomfort afterward. People respond differently, and your surgeon will discuss your health history, medications, and comfort concerns before deciding what’s appropriate. Plan for someone else to drive you home after IV sedation, and follow the specific instructions you receive about food, medication, and recovery.

### Can I really get new teeth in just one day in San Francisco?

Some patients can receive a temporary fixed bridge on surgery day, but same-day teeth aren’t suitable for every case. ### What happens if I have significant bone loss in my jaw?

Significant bone loss doesn’t automatically rule out implant treatment, but it can change the plan. Imaging and an examination help the surgeon assess bone availability and nearby anatomy. Depending on the case, options may include grafting or specialized approaches such as zygomatic implants for certain upper-jaw situations. These techniques aren’t right for everyone, so ask your surgeon to explain the benefits, limitations, and alternatives for your specific anatomy.

### How do I clean and maintain my All-on-4 implants?

Clean the bridge and gumline every day using the tools and technique your care team recommends. Depending on your restoration, that may include a soft toothbrush and an aid for cleaning beneath the bridge, such as an interdental brush or water flosser. Keep professional maintenance appointments even if everything feels normal. Your team can check the fit, bite, and areas that are difficult to reach at home.

Coverage varies by dental plan, and some policies may treat parts of implant treatment differently, such as surgery, extractions, or the prosthesis. ### What is the difference between All-on-4 and a snap-in denture?

All-on-4 generally refers to a fixed full-arch bridge attached to implants, which a dental professional removes for certain maintenance. A snap-in denture attaches to implants for added retention but is designed to be taken out by the wearer for cleaning. Both can replace a full arch, yet they differ in daily handling, feel, and care. Your bite, dexterity, oral health, and preferences help guide the choice.

### How soon can I eat solid foods after the procedure?

Follow your surgeon’s diet instructions rather than using a fixed number of days as a target. While implants heal, the temporary restoration and developing integration need protection from excessive biting forces. Your team may recommend starting with soft foods and advancing gradually based on healing and comfort. Avoid hard, crunchy, or sticky foods until your surgeon says they’re appropriate, and contact the care team if eating causes unexpected pain or movement.
