## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between an implant dentist and a general dentist?

An implant dentist focuses on planning and placing dental implants and coordinating the replacement teeth they support. In California, implant dentistry isn’t a separate formally recognized dental specialty, so the title alone doesn’t establish a particular credential. General dentists commonly provide preventive care and routine treatment, while implant care involves surgical planning. When comparing an implant dentist in Roseville CA, consider the team’s qualifications and experience with cases like yours, whether you’re in Roseville, Rocklin, Loomis, Lincoln, Sacramento, Elk Grove, or El Dorado Hills.

### Does dental insurance cover dental implants in California?

Sometimes, but coverage depends on your specific dental plan and which parts of treatment it includes. A policy may treat the implant, abutment, crown, or related services differently, and plan limits or exclusions can affect payment. Before scheduling, request a written estimate or pre-treatment benefit review from your insurer and ask which parts of the proposed plan are covered. The estimate can help you understand your expected out-of-pocket responsibility, but it isn’t always a guarantee of final payment.

### How long can dental implants last?

Dental implants can serve for many years, but no exact lifespan can be promised for every person. The implant itself and the crown, bridge, or denture it supports are different components, and the restoration may need maintenance or replacement over time. Oral hygiene, general health, and regular dental checkups all matter. Ask your dental team what follow-up care applies to your restoration and how to recognize changes that deserve attention.

### Can dental implants be placed in one day?

Yes, some patients may receive implant treatment on the day of surgery, but that doesn’t mean every case is suitable or that the complete treatment journey ends that day. A clinical evaluation determines whether same-day care is appropriate for your needs. Clarify what “same day” refers to in your proposed plan, such as implant placement or a temporary restoration, and what further care may be needed.

### Can I get dental implants if I smoke?

Smoking doesn’t automatically determine whether you can receive implants, but it’s an important factor for the clinical team to consider. It may affect healing and treatment risks, so be open about whether and how often you smoke. Your overall oral health and treatment needs also influence candidacy. Ask how smoking affects your individual plan and whether changing your smoking habits before or after treatment could support care.

### How do I clean and care for dental implants?

Care for implants with consistent daily cleaning and routine professional dental visits. Brush carefully around the restoration and gumline, and clean between teeth with floss or another tool recommended for your specific restoration. Fixed bridges and removable snap-in dentures can require different cleaning techniques, so ask your dental team to demonstrate the method for your appliance. Contact the team if you notice persistent bleeding, discomfort, or a change in how the restoration fits.

### Does Medicare cover dental implants?

Original Medicare generally doesn’t cover routine dental care, including implants, though limited exceptions may apply when dental services are closely connected to certain covered medical treatment. Medicare Advantage plans may include dental benefits, but coverage varies by plan. Review your plan documents and ask the plan administrator whether implant placement and the associated restoration are covered, what exclusions apply, and whether approval is required. Get the details in writing before making financial decisions about treatment.
