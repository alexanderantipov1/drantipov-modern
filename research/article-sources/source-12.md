## Dental Implants for Missing Teeth in Elk Grove: What Can They Replace?

A missing tooth can affect more than your smile. It may change how you chew, speak, or feel in social situations, and several missing teeth can make everyday routines more challenging. Dental implants for missing teeth Elk Grove patients consider can support different types of tooth replacement, depending on the pattern of tooth loss and individual oral health.

A dental implant is a small post placed in the jawbone to replace a missing tooth root. After it bonds with the surrounding bone, a connecting component called an abutment can attach the implant to the visible restoration. That restoration may be a crown for one tooth, a bridge for several teeth, or a denture supported by implants. For a foundational explanation of [what are dental implants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dental_implant), including how they integrate with bone, see this overview.

### How an implant-supported tooth replacement works

Think of the implant post as the foundation, the abutment as the connector, and the crown, bridge, or denture as the part that restores the visible chewing surface. The post replaces the root; the restoration replaces the part of the tooth or teeth you see and use. These are related but distinct parts of a treatment plan. The stages and timing vary according to the clinical plan, so ask the provider to explain what to expect in your specific case.

### Which missing-tooth situations can implants address?

**One missing tooth:** A single-tooth implant can support an individual crown without using neighboring teeth as the foundation for the replacement. Whether this suits your situation depends on an evaluation of the area and your oral health.

**Several missing teeth:** When multiple teeth are missing, a provider may consider an implant-supported bridge or denture. The number and position of missing teeth help guide planning.

**Most or all teeth missing in an arch:** Full-arch treatment is planned differently from replacing one or a few teeth. Options may include a fixed restoration or a removable implant-supported denture.
