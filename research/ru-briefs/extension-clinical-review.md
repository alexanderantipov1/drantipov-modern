| **P1: Bleeding or swelling alone interpreted as peri-implantitis.** | Health, mucositis and peri-implantitis are distinguished. Mucositis is soft-tissue inflammation without additional supporting bone loss; peri-implantitis requires inflammation plus clinical/radiographic evidence of progressive loss beyond initial remodelling. Baseline records, probing, pocket changes and radiographic comparison matter. Neither a photo nor bleeding alone establishes the diagnosis. | [S5]. No home diagnostic thresholds or automatic diagnosis from a CT image. |
| **P2: Peri-implantitis called the universal/main cause of implant failure.** | It is an important cause of late complications, not the only cause of loss; early integration failure and mechanical problems are distinct. Lack of pain is not reassurance. | [S4–S5]. No prevalence or causal ranking was invented. |
| **P3: Risk factors presented as proven causes for every patient.** | Previous periodontitis, poor plaque control and lack of supportive maintenance are emphasized. Association does not prove causation in an individual; risk modification does not guarantee prevention or cure. |
| **P4: Guaranteed implant rescue or bone regrowth.** | Retention is conditional on suitability. Evidence for particular adjuncts and long-term reconstructive outcomes is limited; no favoured laser/device or universal protocol is promoted. |
| **P5: Antibiotics as sufficient or routinely necessary treatment.** | Mechanical biofilm control, access for hygiene, initial nonsurgical treatment, reassessment, and surgery when indicated are described. Routine systemic antibiotics are not recommended by EFP for peri-implantitis treatment; individual indications belong to the clinician. No drug/dose/course or self-treatment instruction is given. | [S5–S6]. EFP's surgical summary also does not recommend systemic antimicrobials as an adjunct and notes inadequate evidence for local antibiotics. This page does not address every other reason an individual might require antimicrobial treatment. |
| **I1: CBCT at every consultation, infection visit or control.** | Examination, existing records and suitable intraoral radiographs are considered first.
